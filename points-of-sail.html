Words you'll hear on the test
Windward / leewardWindward is the side the wind hits first. Leeward is the downwind side, where the boom sits.
Heading upTurning the bow toward the wind. Push the tiller toward the sail (to leeward). Trim in as you go.
Bearing awayAlso called falling off. Turning the bow away from the wind. Pull the tiller toward you (to windward). Ease out as you go.
Starboard / port tackNamed for the side the wind comes over. On starboard tack the wind hits the starboard side and the boom is to port.
TackingTurning the bow through the wind. "Ready about." "Ready." "Helm's a-lee" (or "Hard a-lee").
JibingTurning the stern through the wind. The boom swings across hard, so control it. "Prepare to jibe." "Ready." "Jibe ho."
LuffingThe sail flutters along its front edge because it is eased too far or the boat is too close to the wind.
By the leeSailing downwind with the wind coming over the same side as the boom. An accidental jibe is close.
Right of wayBoats on opposite tacks: port tack gives way to starboard tack. Same tack: the windward boat gives way to the leeward boat.
